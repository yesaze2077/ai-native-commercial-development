Treat the activation shortfall as an observation to investigate; technical health does not establish product value, and a before/after change does not establish causation.

Use a concrete draft metric contract: activation rate = unique eligible new users who complete the agreed first-value action within seven days of first eligible exposure / unique eligible new users first exposed to this release. The product owner must confirm the first-value event and eligibility rule. Exclude test identities, deduplicate by the agreed stable identity, fix timezone and event-time rules, and count only fully matured seven-day cohorts. Keep the pre-release expectation distinct from the observed result.

The analytics owner validates exposure and completion instrumentation, identity joins, event loss and version binding using permitted data. Record numerator, denominator, cohort dates, sample size/uncertainty, source, release/flags and concurrent campaigns or product changes. Missing joins are a measurement limit, not zero activation. The support owner supplies journey-friction reports; engineering owns reliability/error and cost guardrails.

Use two weekly matured cohorts as a proposed initial observation window, with an explicit review date set from the real rollout date and sample needs. Preserve existing operational abort windows. At review, the product owner records continue, improve the journey, revisit the value assumption, stop, or insufficient evidence. Link accepted work to the existing backlog/spec and release evidence. If causal attribution is needed, plan an ethical controlled comparison or a suitable quasi-experiment; report the current comparison descriptively. This plan does not start an unrequested background monitor or claim a measured outcome.
