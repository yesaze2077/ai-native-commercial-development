Quick passes and cached integration results are insufficient for commercial readiness. The audit should report the verified local scope and list the unverified release requirements; it does not authorize production.

Review whether cached integration evidence still applies to all current covered/transitive inputs, with original run provenance. Then require fresh full declared quick, integration and release checks on the exact candidate, plus project-protected CI and applicable risk gates absent from the profile. Check the real user journey through UI/API/persistence, permissions and negative/recovery paths, relevant visual/accessibility evidence, security/dependencies, and applicable migration/restore or rollback evidence. Obtain the required independent review and recorded human ship decision. A required failed or unrun gate means NO-SHIP for now; mark genuinely inapplicable gates with reasons.

Also bind readiness to the target revision/artifact, configuration, environment, operational monitoring/abort conditions, support ownership and applicable commercial promises. Actual deployment requires its separate authorization and receipt. Real deployed smoke must verify the target version and critical resulting state freshly; a local cache cannot satisfy it. Product outcomes remain pending until observed in a defined owned window.

A planner green only describes selection; a cache green only reuses scoped historical evidence. Neither proves complete acceptance, authenticates human approval nor grants production authority. This is an audit plan and readiness conclusion, not a claim that release checks or deployment have occurred.
