Technical health and product activation are separate outcomes. First verify that the apparent shortfall is real before attributing it to the release.

Define activation against the accepted product promise. For example, if first successful Save is that promise: denominator = eligible new users first exposed to the released onboarding in a weekly cohort; numerator = those users completing one server-confirmed persisted Save within seven days of first exposure. Deduplicate by the agreed stable user identity, exclude only predeclared internal/test traffic, and bind exposure/event definitions to the release and configuration. This is a proposed contract, not observed data. Keep the pre-release expectation as a target and the comparable pre-release cohort as a baseline, with sample sizes and telemetry gaps.

The product owner owns the decision; engineering owns instrumentation verification, and analytics owns the reproducible cohort calculation. Use a complete seven-day activation window per cohort, review four complete weekly cohorts if volume supports it, and preserve the existing immediate reliability/abort window. Inspect funnel drop-off, representative support/user feedback, errors, cost and manual intervention. Missing joins or exposure data mean insufficient evidence, not zero activation.
